The fastest way to make Good Product sound ambitious is to describe everything it could become. A place for updates could also hold tasks. Tasks could become a planner. A planner could use a calendar. Before long, we would be building another application people have to live in all day.

We want to make a few boundaries public while they are still easy to remember.

## We are not building another chat room

Conversation already has plenty of homes. We want Good Product to hold the parts worth finding afterward: the update that explains a project's state, the decision that changes its direction, and the handoff that lets someone else move it forward.

Posts have replies because context needs discussion. That does not mean every discussion needs a channel, a presence indicator, or a typing animation.

## We will not score people's activity

Publishing more updates does not necessarily mean contributing more. Reading fewer posts does not necessarily mean someone is disengaged.

We are not adding individual productivity scores, posting streaks, or leaderboards. Those features would encourage behavior that makes the workspace noisier and the work harder to judge fairly.

We do need operational information: whether an email was delivered, whether a save failed, and whether a page is slow. That helps us run the product. It is a different purpose from ranking the people using it.

## We are keeping the team small for this release

A small team forces choices, but it does not automatically produce a focused product. We still have to say no to work we find interesting.

For 1.0, we are prioritizing reliable drafts, useful project pages, and readable updates. A request can be reasonable and still fall outside that list. When it does, we want to explain the tradeoff rather than promise it for an unspecified future.

## Boundaries can change, with a reason

This is not a claim that our first choices will be right forever. It is a record of what we are optimizing for now.

If we expand the product, we should be able to explain how the addition helps a team share context. “Other tools have it” will not be enough.
