Tomorrow we ship Good Product 1.0. Before we publish the release notes, we wanted to share the work that will be less visible: the ideas we let go of along the way.

When we introduced Good Company in September, our working assumption was that teams needed a better place to write updates. We spent a lot of time on the editor. How quickly could you start? How much formatting was enough? Could a blank page feel less intimidating?

Those questions mattered. But the conversations around the early version kept returning to the reader.

## Publishing was only half the job

Our first homepage was a chronological feed. It was easy to build and familiar to use. It also made a busy project look more important than a quiet one with a decision waiting for attention.

We changed the homepage to group work by project. The most recent update still matters, but it sits beside open decisions and the next handoff. People can see a project's state without reconstructing it from individual posts.

That change cost us a week we had planned to spend on new templates. We would make the same trade again.

## We removed a notification setting

The early version subscribed you to a project after you commented on it. It seemed considerate: you had joined the conversation, so surely you wanted to keep following it.

In practice, a single helpful reply could turn into a lasting obligation. We made following an explicit choice. Replies to your own comment still reach you, but a project's future updates do not arrive unless you ask for them.

## We kept one inconvenient boundary

A draft belongs to its author until it is published. We considered showing unfinished updates to project owners so they could see what was coming. We decided against it.

People need somewhere to think before their work becomes a team artifact. A manager's curiosity is not a good enough reason to make that space ambiguous.

## The next version will change our minds again

These are small decisions, but together they define the product more clearly than a feature count. We're building for the person catching up as carefully as the person posting.

Tomorrow's [1.0 release](/announcing-good-product-1-0/) is our best version of that idea so far.
