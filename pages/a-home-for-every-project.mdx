A project usually has a name before it has a shared place to explain itself. The brief lives in one document, updates arrive in chat, and the next person to join asks for a collection of links.

Good Product starts with a project page: a small, stable home for the context around a piece of work.

## Put the explanation at the top

Every project has a short description and an owner. The description answers two questions: what are we trying to do, and why does it matter?

You can add links to the task tracker, design file, or working document. Those tools remain the places where the detailed work happens. The project page helps someone understand how they fit together.

## Keep updates together

Published updates appear in date order. Each has a permanent link, so you can share a specific change without sending someone to the top of a moving feed.

For a new teammate, the sequence becomes a useful record: what the team expected, what changed, and where the work stands now. For the author, it means an update does not disappear as soon as the next conversation starts.

## Make following intentional

You can follow a project to receive its updates. Opening a page or leaving a reply does not silently subscribe you to every future post.

We want a project to be easy to visit without becoming another inbox commitment. Direct replies and mentions still have their own notification path.

## Start with a little structure

The first version does not have custom dashboards or a collection of widgets. We are starting with a description, an owner, useful links, and updates.

Structured decisions and clearer handoffs will build on that foundation. The project page should remain a place someone can understand in a minute, even as the work behind it gets more complicated.
