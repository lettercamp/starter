A good post should have room for whatever helps tell the story. A few paragraphs, a picture, a useful list, or a link that deserves more attention.

This one is a small tour. Read it as a post, or open its MDX file to see how it's put together.

## Start with the words

Most of a post can be ordinary paragraphs. Use **bold** when a point needs emphasis, *italics* for a quieter aside, and [links](/posts/introducing-lettercamp/) to send readers somewhere useful.

### Give the story some shape

Headings help a reader find their place. A main section introduces an idea; a smaller heading can make room for the details.

#### Leave room for the details

You don't need to use every heading level. A short post might need none at all. Let the length and shape of the story decide.

## Let an image do some talking

![Three overlapping pages gathered under the words Worth keeping.](/images/give-your-best-ideas-a-home.png)

*An illustration, a photograph, or a screenshot can give a reader a different way into the same idea.*

## Make a useful list

A list is a good place for things readers might want to save:

- A clear title that tells people what to expect.
- An opening that gives them a reason to keep reading.
- One example that makes the idea easier to understand.
- A next step, when there is a useful one.

For something sequential, use a numbered list:

1. Write a rough first version.
2. Read it aloud and cut what gets in the way.
3. Add the image or example the story needs.
4. Give it a title you'd want to click.

## Give a thought some space

> The best part of a post is often the sentence someone remembers after they've closed the tab.

A quote can bring in another voice or let you pause on an idea. When you're quoting someone else, include their name and a link to the source.

## Show how something works

Inline code, like `title`, is handy when you're naming a setting. A fenced code block works well for a longer example:

```md
## A small observation

Start with something you've noticed.

- Make it specific.
- Give it an example.
- Leave the reader with something to think about.

> One idea is enough for a good post.
```

You don't need all of these in every story. Pick the pieces that help, and let the writing do the rest.
