On a walk last week, I noticed a handwritten sign in a bakery window: *Back in ten. Dough emergency.*

I had passed that bakery dozens of times. Usually I was answering a message, choosing a podcast, or calculating how late I was going to be. This time, I was simply walking. The sign made me laugh, and I remembered it long after everything else from that morning had slipped away.

## The unassigned part of the day

Nearly every spare minute can be given a job. A walk becomes a chance to catch up on the news. A queue becomes an inbox session. Even a quiet evening can turn into a project to improve our taste, our cooking, or ourselves.

There is nothing wrong with any of that. But I have started to miss the minutes that don't come with instructions.

Those are often the minutes when something unexpected gets in: the color of a doorway, a conversation at the next table, an idea that arrives halfway through washing a plate.

> Leave a little room for the day to surprise you.

## A small experiment

For the next week, I am leaving one short walk unfilled. No headphones, no errands folded into the route, no requirement to return with an insight.

I might notice nothing. That seems like a reasonable outcome too.

The point is to make some space available. A few minutes in which the world gets to choose the subject.

If you try it, start small. Walk around the block. Look at the windows. There may be a dough emergency worth knowing about.
