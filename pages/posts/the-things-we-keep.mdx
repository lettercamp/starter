There is a small box on my bookshelf that contains almost nothing of financial value: a train ticket, two photographs, a restaurant receipt, and a button from a coat I no longer own.

I could explain every object in it. I would have trouble explaining why these particular objects survived when so many others didn't.

## An archive with no filing system

We tend to imagine an archive as something deliberate. Labeled folders. Dates. Someone wearing gloves.

Most personal archives are less organized. They accumulate in kitchen drawers, between book pages, and at the bottom of bags. We keep something because throwing it away feels slightly wrong, then discover years later that it has become a shortcut to a whole afternoon.

The train ticket brings back a station café. The receipt brings back a dinner that lasted until the chairs were stacked around us. The button mostly brings back how much I liked that coat.

## What earns a place

Lately, I have been trying a modest rule: keep the things that bring back a specific story.

- A postcard with an actual message on the back.
- A recipe written in someone else's handwriting.
- A photograph that makes me remember what happened just outside the frame.

This still leaves room for a little nonsense. An archive should be allowed to contain a very good pebble.

## Let the objects do their work

Every so often, I open the box. I don't photograph its contents or make a project of arranging them. I just spend a few minutes remembering.

Then the lid goes back on, and the box returns to the shelf. A small collection of ordinary things, holding more than their size suggests.
