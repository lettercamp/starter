Keeping up with a team should not require keeping a tab open all day. Today we're adding a weekly digest to Good Product: a single email that brings together updates from the projects you follow.

The first version sends on Friday morning in your selected time zone. You can turn it off from your notification settings or directly from the email.

## Organized around projects

A busy feed favors whatever was posted last. The digest groups new updates by project, so you can read one thread of work at a time.

Each entry includes its title, author, and a short excerpt, followed by a link to the full post. We keep the email brief enough to scan and let the workspace carry the detail. We plan to include structured decisions when that feature ships.

This is a recap of published material, not an automatically generated interpretation of what the team meant. The original author remains the source of the update.

## Following is an explicit choice

You only receive material from projects you follow and can still access when the digest is prepared. Commenting on a post does not subscribe you to the whole project.

Mentions and direct requests use the normal inbox flow. The digest does not replace those notifications or make an urgent request wait until Friday.

If nothing has changed in your followed projects, we skip the email. An empty recap does not need a place in anyone's inbox.

## A few details we cared about

Dates use the recipient's time zone. Long titles wrap rather than disappearing behind an ellipsis. Links are descriptive enough to make sense with a screen reader. And the unsubscribe action changes only the digest preference, leaving direct notifications alone.

For this first release, delivery is weekly. Choosing another day or a daily schedule is not supported yet. We want to learn whether the recap is useful before adding a calendar's worth of settings.

If you are curious about the infrastructure behind delivery, we wrote about [the notification queue](/why-we-rebuilt-our-notification-queue/) and the failure case that made us rethink it.
